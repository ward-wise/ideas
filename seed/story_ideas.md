## A block brief for door-to-door canvassers

### Who it's for

A volunteer or staffer knocking doors for a campaign, a ballot measure or an organizing drive.

### The question they need answered

"What does this block care about, and what has changed here?" Before walking a turf, a canvasser wants a one-screen brief on a handful of blocks: the few measures where the area stands out from the rest of the ward (best and worst), what moved in the last few years, and plain-language prompts they can use at the door. Examples: "311 pothole requests here take twice the city median," or "this is one of the ward's most rent-burdened areas." It should work on a phone, outdoors, from an address or a drawn turf.

This brief is non-partisan. It reports measures and changes, not how to persuade anyone, and it never shows anything about individual residents.

### Measures it would use

Small-area (χGRID) and community-area values: 311 response times, rent burden, new housing, crime and perceived safety, transit and grocery distance, park access, turnout, plus year-over-year change for each.

## A loan-readiness report for one business idea at one address

### Who it's for

Someone opening or expanding a small business who needs to show a lender (a bank, a CDFI, an SBIF grant reviewer) why this business fits this location.

### The question they need answered

"Why is this business, at this address, a reasonable bet?" Today /for/financing gives a printable page for a whole community area. This report goes one step further: the borrower picks a business type and an address, and it shows the evidence a lender asks for, near that spot, with sources:

- demand: residents, income, and how fast it is changing
- foot traffic and transit nearby
- how many similar businesses are close by, and how many opened and closed recently
- rent and vacancy signals
- the area's track record with city grants and loans

It ends with an honest "what a lender may push back on" section, the way the rankings page lists what undercuts each ranking.

### Measures it would use

Business openings and closures, licensed business mix and chain share, CTA and Divvy activity, income and rent, SBIF grants, crime, and an address-level competitor count (new: same business-license type within a walk).

### Related pages already on Ward Wise

- https://www.wardwise.org/for/financing (community-area version, printable)
- https://www.wardwise.org/for/business
- https://www.wardwise.org/near

## A participatory audit: neighbors build their shared index

### Who it's for

A block club, a neighborhood association or a participatory-budgeting committee that wants a wellbeing measure its own members chose.

### The question they need answered

"Which measures matter to each of us, and what single index best reflects what we share?" Neighbors each pick and rank the measures they care about, on a phone at a meeting or on their own beforehand. The tool shows where the group agrees and where it splits, then proposes a composite: weights drawn from everyone's choices, with each person able to see how far it sits from their own. The group can adjust and save it as a named index, then track it for their area year after year and take it to their alderperson.

Open design questions:
- how to combine rankings fairly (average weights, MaxDiff-style scores, or a vote on the final weights)
- whether to keep individual answers private by default
- how to show disagreement honestly instead of averaging it away

This is the tool behind the "Host a block audit" card on /get-involved.

### Measures it would use

Any measure in the dictionary. The composite builds on the map's existing weights and curated lists.

### Related pages already on Ward Wise

- https://www.wardwise.org/map (weights, curated lists, weighted composite over time)
- https://www.wardwise.org/index (every ranking, with what undercuts it)
- https://www.wardwise.org/get-involved#know (Host a block audit)
